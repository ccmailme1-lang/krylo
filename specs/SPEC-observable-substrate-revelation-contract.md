# SPEC II — KRYLO Real-World Observable Substrate & Lens Revelation Specification

Status: LOCKED — Engineering Review
Depends on: Spec I — locked perceptual / interface model
Role: Real-world observable substrate and revelation model
Scope: What each Lens is capable of revealing from observable reality
Explicitly out of scope: Cone bands, band colors, halo geometry, HUD widgets, final Lens layouts, implementation architecture, final data-provider contracts, mathematical scoring formulas
Reference: KRYL-1209 (Engineering Review Package), SPEC-six-lens-perceptual-experience-contract.md

## 1. Purpose

Define the real-world observable substrate for each of KRYLO's six Lenses and the revelation logic that every Lens must follow.

A Lens is legitimate only if it can:

- start from tangible things that exist in the world;
- surface observations of those things;
- raise legitimate questions;
- expose conditions and relationships;
- contribute to recognition of formations;
- ground perception in evidence;
- hold what remains unresolved.

The six Lenses are not analytical categories or sector dashboards.

They are six different observational cuts through the same world.

## 2. Canonical Revelation Flow

Normative

Every Lens follows:

DOMAIN → OBSERVATION → QUESTION → CONDITION → RELATIONSHIP → FORMATION → EVIDENCE → UNRESOLVED

| Stage | Definition |
|---|---|
| DOMAIN | The tangible substrate the Lens represents |
| OBSERVATION | What happened or exists in the world |
| QUESTION | What the observation legitimately causes us to ask |
| CONDITION | The state or pressure the observation indicates |
| RELATIONSHIP | Entities, events, or other observations that become connected |
| FORMATION | Larger structural configuration that may be emerging or already present |
| EVIDENCE | Material that establishes, corroborates, qualifies, or contradicts the interpretation |
| UNRESOLVED | What cannot yet be established |

**Critical distinctions**

- OBSERVATION ≠ EVIDENCE. An observed event is not automatically proof of its own meaning.
- OBSERVATION ≠ FORMATION. An observed event or movement does not automatically constitute a Formation.
- UNRESOLVED is a legitimate output. The system is not required to force a conclusion.

## 3. Six Lenses as Observational Cuts Through One World

Established principle

The six Lenses do not partition reality into six independent domains.

They provide six observational cuts through the same underlying world.

A real-world event may produce observable consequences across multiple Lenses simultaneously.

The Lenses therefore overlap by design.

Their boundaries define what is being observed and from which perceptual position, not separate territories of reality.

**Example**

A single acquisition may produce:

- OWNERSHIP — control changes;
- CAPITAL — capital is deployed or reallocated;
- TECHNOLOGY — a capability or platform changes control;
- KNOWLEDGE — intellectual property or specialized expertise moves;
- LABOR — organizational capacity or skill composition changes;
- MEDIA — attention and narrative change.

These are not six interpretations of the same event.

They are six potentially observable consequences of the same event.

**Cross-Domain Observation**

A relationship between domains is itself observable.

Therefore: one observation can produce, connect to, or constrain observations in another domain.

Cross-domain propagation is consequently not an analytical afterthought.

It is part of the observable substrate.

**Lens Boundary**

A Lens establishes an observational boundary, not an ontological boundary.

Its purpose is to answer: What can be observed from this cut through reality, and what does that observation reveal when connected to what is happening elsewhere?

This prevents the six Lenses from becoming six conventional sector dashboards.

## 4. Core Principles

- Domains are tangible substrates, not abstract analytical categories.
- Each substrate produces observable traces that can be sourced externally.
- Lenses provide different perceptual cuts through the same world; they do not partition reality.
- A single real-world event can generate observations across multiple domains.
- Cross-domain relationships are themselves observable.
- Revelation must be able to move from perception back to underlying observations and provenance.
- Money movement and similar consequential activity are observable classes, not Lenses.
- Surface, halo, and layout decisions remain downstream of this specification.

## 5. CAPITAL

**Domain**: Movement, allocation, financing, and deployment of capital.

**Observable Objects**: funds; ETFs; institutional portfolios; corporate balance sheets; financing vehicles; capital commitments; debt instruments; equity issuances.

**Observation Classes**: sector/fund/ETF flows; financing rounds; debt issuances; capital calls; buybacks; dividends; large-scale reallocations; measurable changes in capital intensity; deployment velocity changes.

**Question**: Where is capital moving, what is changing around that movement, and what does the movement make possible or constrain?

**Conditions**: concentration; scarcity; deployment velocity; liquidity pressure; financing stress; strategic reallocation.

**Relationships**: Capital sources ↔ recipients ↔ intermediaries ↔ asset classes ↔ linked ownership, technology, labor, or knowledge shifts.

**Formation Contribution**: Potential structural formations include capital structures; funding clusters; defensive liquidity positioning; cross-border capital realignment; capital formation around new capability or infrastructure classes. A single capital movement does not automatically constitute a Formation.

**Evidence**: flow data; transaction records; capital-structure disclosures; fund filings; contemporaneous observations from other domains.

**Unresolved**: stated purpose versus actual deployment; secondary effects not yet visible; incomplete disclosure; conflicting signals regarding sustainability.

**Cross-Domain Propagation**: Capital movement can generate or connect to observations in Ownership (control financed), Technology (capability funded), Labor (capacity expanded or contracted), Knowledge (research financed), Media (attention to capital events).

## 6. TECHNOLOGY

**Domain**: Technological capability, adoption, displacement, and infrastructure.

**Observable Objects**: software platforms; technical standards; patents; infrastructure assets; corporate technical activity; deployment footprints.

**Observation Classes**: platform launches or retirements; adoption or displacement signals; infrastructure build-outs or decommissionings; patent activity; technical disclosures; measurable shifts in technological intensity.

**Question**: What capability is being introduced, adopted, or displaced, what is changing around it, and what does the change make possible or obsolete?

**Conditions**: adoption momentum; displacement pressure; capability concentration; infrastructure readiness or lag; technological dependency.

**Relationships**: Technology ↔ adopters ↔ displaced alternatives ↔ enabling infrastructure ↔ capital required ↔ skills required ↔ ownership of key assets or IP.

**Formation Contribution**: Potential structural formations include platform consolidation; infrastructure bottlenecks; capability clusters; displacement cascades.

**Evidence**: deployment data; patent records; technical disclosures; adoption metrics; infrastructure announcements; cross-domain corroboration.

**Unresolved**: claimed versus actual capability; adoption durability; secondary displacement effects; proprietary infrastructure opacity.

**Cross-Domain Propagation**: Technology events can surface in Capital (funding of capability), Ownership (control of IP or platforms), Knowledge (technical knowledge transfer), Labor (skill-demand shifts), Media (attention to new or displaced capability).

## 7. KNOWLEDGE

**Domain**: Creation, transfer, concentration, and dissemination of knowledge.

**Observable Objects**: publications; patents; research institutions; funders; awards; collaboration networks; open repositories; specialized talent.

**Observation Classes**: publication or patent events; collaboration or dissolution events; knowledge-transfer agreements; talent movement carrying specialized knowledge; open releases or withdrawals; measurable concentration or diffusion of expertise.

**Question**: Where is knowledge being created, concentrated, or moved, what is changing around it, and what does the change make possible or constrain?

**Conditions**: knowledge concentration; diffusion rate; transfer friction; expertise scarcity or surplus; institutional capture.

**Relationships**: Producers ↔ carriers (people, documents, code) ↔ institutional holders ↔ capital or technology that amplifies the knowledge ↔ labor markets that depend on it.

**Formation Contribution**: Potential structural formations include expertise clusters; knowledge bottlenecks; rapid diffusion pathways; institutional monopolization of critical knowledge.

**Evidence**: publications; citations; patents; collaboration records; funding disclosures; talent-flow data; cross-domain signals.

**Unresolved**: tacit versus explicit knowledge; actual transfer success; durability of concentration; conflicting dissemination signals.

**Cross-Domain Propagation**: Knowledge events can appear in Technology (capability enabled), Labor (skill embodiment), Capital (research financing), Ownership (control of IP), Media (attention to discoveries or controversies).

## 8. LABOR

**Domain**: People, skills, employment, and organizational capacity.

**Observable Objects**: workers; occupational categories; employers; facilities; training institutions; wage data; job-opening data.

**Observation Classes**: hiring or layoff events at scale; facility openings or closures; occupational demand shifts; labor-action events; training-capacity changes; measurable headcount or skill-mix shifts; geographic workforce redistribution.

**Question**: Where is human capacity moving, concentrating, or being released, what is changing around it, and what does the change make possible or constrain?

**Conditions**: skill scarcity or surplus; organizational capacity expansion or contraction; labor-market pressure; geographic concentration or dispersion.

**Relationships**: Workers ↔ employers ↔ skill requirements ↔ locations ↔ capital or technology that substitutes or complements labor ↔ ownership structures that control capacity.

**Formation Contribution**: Potential structural formations include skill clusters; capacity bottlenecks; organizational restructuring patterns; cross-border labor realignments.

**Evidence**: employment filings; facility announcements; occupational and wage data; training-capacity signals; cross-domain observations.

**Unresolved**: quality versus quantity of capacity; actual skill-transfer outcomes; secondary effects; incomplete internal visibility.

**Cross-Domain Propagation**: Labor events can connect to Capital (cost or investment implications), Technology (automation or complementary capability), Knowledge (embodied expertise movement), Ownership (control of organizational capacity), Media (attention/narrative around employment or labor conflict).

## 9. MEDIA

**Domain**: Public attention, narrative, communication, and information propagation.

**Observable Objects**: stories; events; entities; attention metrics; narrative clusters; platforms; communication campaigns.

**Observation Classes**: measurable attention shifts; narrative launches or collapses; propagation events; platform or ownership changes that alter propagation; coordinated or emergent narrative formations.

**Question**: What is receiving attention, how is the narrative forming or fracturing, what is changing around it, and what does the change make more or less possible?

**Conditions**: attention concentration or diffusion; narrative coherence or contestation; propagation velocity; information asymmetry.

**Relationships**: Attention sources ↔ amplifiers ↔ audiences ↔ contested narratives ↔ underlying events in other domains that the narrative interprets or obscures.

**Formation Contribution**: Potential structural formations include attention monopolies; narrative polarization structures; propagation pathways that systematically favor or suppress certain observations.

**Evidence**: attention metrics; narrative content and timing; platform changes; relationships between media signals and observable events in other domains.

**Unresolved**: intentional versus emergent narrative; actual influence on other domains; durability of attention; conflicting signals across channels.

**Cross-Domain Propagation**: Media events can amplify or obscure observations originating in any of the other five domains and can themselves become inputs to Capital (sentiment-sensitive flows), Ownership (attention to control changes), Technology (attention to capabilities), Knowledge (attention to discoveries), Labor (attention to workforce events).

## 10. OWNERSHIP

**Domain**: Control, possession, acquisition, disposition, and institutional boundaries.

**Observable Objects**: legal entities; equity and asset holdings; beneficial owners; transaction records; corporate control instruments; institutional boundaries.

**Observation Classes**: acquisitions; divestitures; mergers; changes in control; equity or asset transfers; institutional boundary shifts; new entities; spin-outs; consolidations; measurable changes in ownership concentration.

**Question**: What changed hands or control, what is changing around that shift, and what does the change make possible or constrain?

**Conditions**: control concentration or diffusion; ownership stress; institutional re-bounding; strategic repositioning of control.

**Relationships**: Acquirer ↔ target ↔ capital sources ↔ controlled assets (technology, knowledge, labor, etc.) ↔ regulatory or jurisdictional boundaries.

**Formation Contribution**: Potential structural formations include vertical consolidation; horizontal consolidation; platform control structures; cross-domain ownership clusters; defensive ownership repositioning.

**Evidence**: SEC filings; beneficial-ownership disclosures; transaction records; control-change announcements; subsequent asset/control changes; cross-domain corroboration.

**Unresolved**: ultimate beneficial control; secondary effects not yet visible; regulatory outcomes; incomplete or conflicting disclosure.

**Cross-Domain Propagation**: Ownership events frequently originate observations in Capital (financing of the transaction), Technology (capability changing hands), Knowledge (IP or expertise transfer), Labor (organizational-capacity change), Media (attention and narrative).

## 11. Canonical Cross-Domain Test — M&A

An M&A transaction is not a Lens. It is a real-world event that can generate observations across all six domains.

**OWNERSHIP** — Observation: Company A announces acquisition of Company B. Condition: Control concentration increasing. Formation contribution: Potential horizontal consolidation across a capability category.

**CAPITAL** — Observation: Transaction financing, payment structure, or related capital movement. Condition: Capital deployed or reallocated to effect control change.

**TECHNOLOGY** — Observation: Capability, platform, or infrastructure changes hands. Condition: Technological dependency or concentration shifts.

**KNOWLEDGE** — Observation: Specialized knowledge, IP, or research capacity transfers or becomes controlled. Condition: Knowledge concentration or transfer friction.

**LABOR** — Observation: Organizational capacity, headcount, or skill mix changes. Condition: Capacity expansion, contraction, or reorganization pressure.

**MEDIA** — Observation: Attention and narrative form around the transaction. Condition: Narrative coherence or contestation regarding the meaning of the control change.

**Evidence** across the chain may include: merger filings; transaction value; ownership disclosures; financing documents; subsequent asset/control changes; contemporaneous signals from other domains.

**Unresolved** potential elements include: whether anticipated capability concentration actually materializes; whether control changes produce the expected structural effects; regulatory outcomes; secondary effects not yet visible.

This test demonstrates that the Lenses function as perceptual cuts through a shared world rather than isolated dashboards.

## 12. Evidence & Provenance Requirement

Every perception that moves beyond raw observation must be able to point back to:

- the underlying observation(s);
- the evidence that supports or qualifies the interpretation;
- the residual unresolved elements.

This requirement is independent of any particular visual surface.

The system must therefore preserve the path from interpreted perception back to observable reality and provenance.

## 13. Validation Tests

The revelation architecture was tested against four materially different event classes.

| Event class | Domain entry | Observation ≠ Evidence | Observation ≠ Formation | Unresolved held | Cross-domain propagation | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M&A | Ownership | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | PASS |
| Platform / capability displacement | Technology | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | PASS |
| Financing / reallocation without ownership change | Capital | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | PASS |
| Workforce / facility restructuring | Labor | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | PASS |

**Validation Test 1 — Technology**: A platform/capability displacement event can enter through Technology, generate observable adoption/displacement signals, expose conditions and relationships, contribute to a Formation only when sufficiently supported, and preserve unresolved questions around durability and actual capability. Result: PASS.

**Validation Test 2 — Capital**: A major financing or capital-reallocation event can enter directly through Capital without requiring an ownership change. Result: PASS.

**Validation Test 3 — Labor**: A workforce/facility restructuring can enter directly through Labor and expose organizational capacity, skills, geographic movement, and cross-domain relationships without automatically becoming a Formation. Result: PASS.

The architecture therefore accommodates events originating in different domains without distortion.

**Validation scope distinction**: These are internal design/reasoning tests against representative real-world event classes. The separate live-code grounding audit (KRYL-1209) did not execute or independently verify these tests — it assessed the current implementation for code presence, wiring, reachability, and related lexical/conceptual/behavioral evidence. The two tracks are independent.

## 14. What Remains Downstream

The following are deliberately not defined by Spec II:

- exact cone-surface encodings;
- band geometry;
- color systems;
- halo composition;
- spatial layout;
- specific HUD elements;
- final data-provider contracts;
- scoring or weighting formulas;
- implementation architecture.

These decisions are downstream of the observable-substrate and revelation model.

## 15. Engineering Boundary

Spec II establishes what the Lens system must be capable of revealing.

It does not prescribe how Engineering must implement that capability.

Engineering may determine: appropriate data structures; provider integrations; ingestion mechanisms; normalization; event handling; storage; derivation; performance mechanisms; implementation architecture.

However, implementation must preserve the normative distinctions established by this specification.

In particular, implementation must not collapse:

- Observation → Evidence
- Observation → Formation

and must preserve Unresolved as a valid state.

The implementation must also preserve the ability to trace interpreted perception back to its underlying observations and evidence.
