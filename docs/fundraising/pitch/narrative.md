## 1. Opening

Every major structural shift was visible in pieces before it was obvious as a whole. The signals
were there — in filings, hiring data, patents, research, prices, news. They were in different
places, organized by source, and nobody had them assembled.

## 2. The problem

Decision-makers don't lack information. They lack a way to see **which separate signals are
becoming related.** Today that work is human: collect, read, compare, connect, hypothesize,
validate, repeat. It doesn't scale, and it's bounded by the analyst's existing model.

Dashboards answer *what is happening to this metric.* AI research tools produce coherent
explanations — but coherence isn't proof the relationship is real.

## 3. What KRYLO does

KRYLO observes distributed evidence across six domains — technology, capital, knowledge, labor,
media, ownership — identifies relationships between signals, and tests whether they constitute an
emerging structural formation. Every relationship it presents carries the evidence that admitted
it.

```
SIGNAL → RELATIONSHIP → FORMATION → PROPAGATION │ the customer interprets and decides
```

We don't predict. We detect. KRYLO presents the map; the customer decides what it means.

## 4. Why it generalizes

The subject changes — semiconductors, biopharma, automotive, logistics, media, government. The
problem doesn't: the answer is distributed across relationships. One engine, many subjects.
(`docs/market/problem-markets.md`)

## 5. Who buys

People whose job is detecting change: investment research, corporate strategy, government and
resilience, demographic strategy, technology strategy. (`docs/go-to-market/gtm-strategy.md`)
